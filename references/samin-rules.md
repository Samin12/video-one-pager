## Standing rules

1. **The one-pager is written before filming.** Always use the planning voice, even for a video that's
   already out.
2. **His fields, verbatim:** Video idea, Goal of video, Key things to create, Intro, Why this matters,
   Level 1-3, CTA END. Nothing extra unless he adds it.
3. **Every point gets an example, story or analogy.** That's "how I'm going to make this easy".
4. **Step by step only where he screenshares.** Concept points have no steps.
5. **Use his material first:** board cards, his Why this matters drafts, prompts he already ran, lines
   from his past videos.
6. **Placeholders, not inventions**, for results, costs and his own pipeline steps.
7. **Verify facts** (prices, limits) against current sources, and cite them in chat.
8. **He revises by marking up the page.** Fold in every note, keep his words, keep his deletions, and
   hand back only the outline.
9. **Dictation:** read it in context and trust typed words over dictated ones.
10. **Script:** spoken lines are prose, anything shown on screen is a bullet, one block per section, and
    the cards on Heptabase run left to right.
11. **A variation is a new card** under the original block, in the same column.
12. **When he says "slide" or "visual"**, write the image prompt as well.
13. **CTA END sends people to his next video.** No recap unless he asks.
14. **On his board:** re-read the layout before placing anything (he moves sections around), never
    overwrite a card of his, and never test a board-changing command with input that might be valid.
15. **Start from his drafts.** If his board already has a hook or intro card, script from it and change
    only what the one-pager changed. He put his own hook back over a newly written one.
16. **Visual cues he isn't presenting go inline** in square brackets inside the spoken line:
    "[show montage of cool clips]", "[show analytics from my YouTube and Instagram]", "[char ref sheet]".
    Bullets are for what he presents or walks through: screenshare steps, demos, slides.
17. **Keep lines short:** one or two sentences per line, with long paragraphs split up.
18. **Say each thing once** and cut lines that repeat it.
19. **Why this matters is talk.** Demos belong in the levels.
20. **When he picks a variation**, he renames it, swaps it into the row, and folds the old block. Leave
    his layout the way he set it.
21. **When the fix is a setup, name the whole setup** ("ChatGPT + Genjutsu"), not just the one tool.
